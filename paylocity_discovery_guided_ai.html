Discovery Playbook

Diagnose → Quantify → Align → Advance

The spine (every first meeting)

  1. Context. Why are we talking? Source + what made it worth their time.
  2. Change posture. Curious, frustrated, evaluating, or committed?
  3. Why now. Event, risk, growth, renewal, or leadership priority.
  4. Current state. How HR/payroll works across systems, people, spreadsheets.
  5. Friction. Where is the team doing work technology should handle?
  6. Impact. Time, errors, risk, employee experience, manager productivity, visibility.
  7. Future state. What must improve for switching to be worth it?
  8. Buying path. Who cares, who approves, who blocks, what must be validated?

Close every discovery

"What I'm hearing is…" → current state → top 3 problems → business impact → desired future state → next best step.

The 5 rules

  1. Respect the incumbent before diagnosing gaps.
  2. Every pain needs consequence, impact, and priority.
  3. Match language to persona: HR, payroll, finance, IT, executive.
  4. Don't demo until you can summarize the problem better than the buyer.
  5. The next meeting solves the next buying problem, not your next sales step.

Frame (0–3 min)

"Rather than jump into Paylocity, I'd like to understand what prompted the conversation and whether there are areas where doing something differently could create value."

Origin + posture (3–7 min)

"What made this worth having?" → "Are you exploring, or has the business already decided something needs to change?"

Current state + friction (7–15 min)

"Walk me through how HR and payroll work today." → "Where would I see your team doing work they shouldn't have to do?"

Opener by source

Partner referral
"What was the partner hearing that made this worthwhile?" Validate partner intel; don't assume urgency.
SDR / telemarketing
"What came out of that conversation that made this worth exploring?" Separate curiosity from a project.
Outbound / marketing
"What caught your attention enough to take the meeting?" Respect that they may not be shopping.

12 questions that open the deal (a tree, not a checklist)

  1. What made this conversation worth having?
  2. Are you exploring, or has change already been decided?
  3. Why is this coming up now?
  4. What works well today?
  5. Where is work happening outside the system?
  6. Where do employees or managers still need HR as the middleman?
  7. What lives in Excel that should live in HCM?
  8. If your team could fix one process tomorrow, what would it be?
  9. What happens when that process breaks?
  10. What would your CFO or CEO want improved?
  11. What changes in the next 12–24 months?
  12. What must be better for change to be worth it?

When they say something meaningful, slow down

Clarify→Evidence→Consequence→Impact→Priority→Future state

Branch cards: match their exact words

"Service is terrible"
  • What does that look like? Recent example?
  • Who chases resolution?
  • What breaks when issues cross modules?
"We're pretty happy"
  • What do they do well?
  • What would you improve anyway?
  • What could become limiting as the business changes?
"Just looking"
  • Why explore now? What are you comparing?
  • What would move this from educational to serious?
"Payroll hates it"
  • What specifically frustrates them?
  • Which process causes the most friction?
  • What if that person leaves?
"CFO told us to look"
  • What got their attention: cost, controls, labor visibility, reporting, risk?
  • What will they need to see?
"Reporting is poor"
  • What report? For whom?
  • How is it built today?
  • What decisions depend on it?
"Too many systems"
  • Where is the employee master record?
  • Where is duplicate entry?
  • What happens when data mismatches?
"We need AI / automation"
  • What repetitive work should disappear?
  • Who uses it?
  • What data and governance are required?
"Implementation scares us"
  • Which part creates resistance: data, payroll accuracy, integrations, timing, resources, change management?
"Price matters"
  • Are you reducing spend or validating value?
  • What's the total cost beyond the invoice?
  • How does Finance judge ROI?
"Managers struggle"
  • What tasks do they avoid? Where does HR step in?
  • Usability, training, accountability, or capability?
"We need to move quickly"
  • What drives the date?
  • What has already been decided?
  • What must be validated before commitment?

Same problem, different value language

HR · time, experience, compliance
  • What consumes more HR time than it should?
  • Where is HR the middleman?
  • Which part of the employee lifecycle feels disconnected?
  • What are managers unable or unwilling to do themselves?
  • What would leadership say HR needs to improve next year?
Payroll · accuracy, control, resilience
  • What creates the most stress before payroll closes?
  • Where does payroll still need manual intervention?
  • What causes corrections after payroll?
  • How dependent are you on one person's knowledge?
  • What would make payroll less dependent on heroics?
Finance · visibility, cost, ROI
  • What labor or people data is hard to get?
  • How are payroll and HR data converted for Finance?
  • How much manual GL or reconciliation work exists?
  • If cost is higher but labor and risk drop, how is ROI judged?
IT · security, integration, governance
  • Where does employee data need to move?
  • Which integrations are business-critical?
  • What security, SSO, MFA, provisioning and audit requirements matter?
  • What architecture is easier to support three years from now?
CEO / Owner · growth, risk, execution
  • What made this important enough for you?
  • What people issue concerns you as the business grows?
  • Where is HR/payroll holding execution back?
  • If this succeeds, what changes in the business?

Let the customer build the competitive argument

"If you were selecting a system today with everything you now know, would you choose the same provider again?"
ADP · scale + depth
  • What originally made ADP the right choice?
  • Where has the experience not kept pace?
  • When an issue crosses payroll, time, tax and benefits, who owns resolution?
  • How much tribal knowledge does administration require?
  • How quickly can Finance get unplanned labor/headcount answers?
Paycom · employee-driven workflows
  • How well has the employee-driven model worked in practice?
  • Where does HR still step back into the process?
  • When employees enter something incorrectly, who catches and corrects it?
  • Has self-service reduced HR workload, or just shifted it?
  • Where do you need more flexibility as the business changes?
Paychex · payroll-centric fit
  • What has kept you with Paychex?
  • What worked at 50 employees that is harder at 150?
  • Which processes have moved outside the core platform?
  • When service transfers happen, does context follow?
  • Where are you trying to mature beyond payroll?
UKG · configurable workforce management
  • What does UKG handle especially well?
  • Where does configurability create admin complexity?
  • Who owns configuration today?
  • Do any workflows still reflect implementation-era design?
  • Do managers use it intuitively, or need HR intervention?
Rippling · modern automation
  • What automation has delivered the biggest benefit?
  • Where has the business needed nuance beyond standard workflow?
  • When payroll is urgent, what does escalation look like?
  • How has pricing evolved as modules were added?
PEO · outsourced infrastructure
  • What originally drove you into the PEO? Does that reason still exist?
  • Do you know the total annual relationship cost?
  • What decisions do you want more direct control over?
  • If you left, how easily could you retrieve employee history and documents?
Fragmented stack
  • How did the environment get here?
  • Which systems work well enough that you wouldn't replace them?
  • Where is the employee master record?
  • How many systems need updates when employee data changes?
  • Where do mismatches or logins confuse employees or managers?
Legacy / homegrown
  • What has kept this working for so long?
  • Where is the organization outgrowing it?
  • Who understands how everything works?
  • How hard is it to add modern tools, APIs, SSO, mobile, AI and security?
  • If designing from scratch today, would you build it this way?

Use when you hear "manual," "slow," "messy," "errors"

  • Frequency: Per payroll? Per month? Per hire?
  • Roles: Who gets pulled in: HR, payroll, managers, finance, IT?
  • Time: How much time does each occurrence take?
  • Risk: What happens if it's wrong, late, or missed?
  • Visibility: Who needs the answer, and how fast?
  • Priority: Relative to everything else, how important is fixing this?
  • Status quo: What happens if nothing changes for 12–24 months?
  • Success: What would make the disruption of switching worth it?

Live math

Manual work = frequency × time × loaded hourly cost
= $22,880 / year
Error cost = occurrences × correction time × role cost + employee impact. Add payroll trust, compliance and escalation where relevant.
Tool sprawl = license cost + admin time + integration maintenance. Show total cost of ownership, not just the invoice.
Decision delay = time to produce data × decisions affected × business consequence. Useful for reporting, labor visibility, turnover and headcount.

Don't default to a demo: solve the next buying problem

Pain unclear
Workflow discovery. Map the employee lifecycle and the manual work.
Pain but no value
Build the impact model. Quantify time, cost, risk and the status quo.
Value, stakeholders absent
Multi-thread. Bring Finance, IT and Exec in through their own lens.
Stakeholders aligned
Targeted demo. Show only what maps to validated problems.
Selection active
Mutual action plan. Dates, validation points, decision process.

Deal standards

  • No proposal until at least one problem has a quantified or defensible business impact.
  • MEDDPICC exit test: you can write the business case in their language, with their numbers and their risks.

After the call

  • Recap in customer language. Use Build recap email.
  • Update MEDDPICC and name the next buying problem.
  • AI prompt: "Help me diagnose, not pitch. Identify missing evidence, quantification gaps, stakeholder risk, and the next best question."
